The 30-second version. DataCo order items → 62,011 locatable orders → 337 bounded-size demand zones → weekly forecasts of orders and units (simple break-aware models won) → exact CFLP (Pyomo + CBC) opens 6 CONUS and 2 Puerto Rico warehouses, proven optimal → GA, K-Means and random are all worse → CVRP per depot with OR-Tools Guided Local Search: 16 vans, 31,610 km/week, all constraints met → forecasting lowered CONUS planning cost by 29.5% but raised Puerto Rico's by 21.6% (biased units forecast).

Key equations

CFLP  min Z = Σj FjYj + ΣiΣj c·dij·oi·Xij   s.t. ΣjXij=1 ∀i;  ΣiuiXij ≤ QjYj ∀j;  Xij ≤ Yj;  pmin ≤ ΣYj ≤ pmax;  X,Y ∈ {0,1}.
c = $0.10/order-km; F = $1,500/wk (PR $1,200); Q = 80 units/wk (PR 100); p ∈ [1,12] (PR [1,4]); d = 1.2 × great-circle km. Orders oi → cost; units ui → capacity.
GA fitness  f(y) = ΣjFjyj + transport(A(y)) + M·unserved(y),  M = $10,000/unit; A(y) = capacity-feasible regret allocation; best y re-allocated exactly by CBC.
CVRP  min ΣkΣiΣj dijxijk   s.t. each stop once (or penalised drop, reported); start/end at depot; load ≤ 30 packages; route ≤ 3,000 km (PR 400); ≤ 8 vans/depot.
GLS  utility(a) = ca/(1+pa);  g(s) = cost(s) + λ Σa pa Ia(s);  10 s per depot; best true-cost solution returned.
XGBoost  L = Σ l(y,ŷ) + Σk[γT + ½λ‖w‖²];  wj* = −Gj/(Hj+λ);  gain = ½[GL²/(HL+λ) + GR²/(HR+λ) − (GL+GR)²/(HL+HR+λ)] − γ.
Metrics  WAPE = Σ|e|/Σy  ·  Cost reduction % = (baseline − optimised)/baseline × 100  ·  Utilisation = used/available × 100.

Slide-by-slide talking points

1 Title. Two linked decisions: where the warehouses go, and how the vans run. The background is the real GLS route output.
2 Problem. Location sets the cost base, capacity limits the allocation, routes cost money every week, and demand moves. Solved in sequence, not jointly.
3 Objectives. Eight objectives, each measured against baselines on identical inputs. The contribution is integration plus honest benchmarking.
4 Cleaning. Items → orders, because one order is one delivery. Cancelled and fraud orders never ship (−2,855). Bad coordinates are not repaired (−886). PII dropped. 37/37 checks. Rows flagged, not deleted.
5 Zones. Complete linkage bounds the zone diameter (50 / 10 km), and it's deterministic. These are customer coordinates, not delivery addresses. Puerto Rico is separate.
6 Architecture. Strategic row → operational row, connected by real files. python main.py all; 54 min; 69 tests.
7 Forecasting. Weekly; orders and units separate; 4 methods; 29 leakage-tested features. The break took units/order from 6.5 to 1. XGBoost lost to simple, break-aware methods.
8 CFLP math. Y = open, X = assign. Objective = fixed + transport. Constraints: assign once, capacity, linking, count. Handling is a constant.
9 Solver. 37 candidates (coverage + demand); Pyomo; CBC branch-and-cut; optimal = bound equals incumbent; independent recomputation of costs.
10 GA. 37-bit chromosome; tournament, two-point crossover, bit-flip mutation, repair, elitism; 5 seeds. Convergence ≠ optimality.
11 Results. CFLP best everywhere. GA +0.58%, K-Means +0.85%, random +62.8% (CONUS). In Puerto Rico, K-Means +27.8% because it ignores capacity.
12 Sites. 6 sites: the marginal site saves less than its $1,500. Brooklyn and Long Beach are near full, so demand overflows. Puerto Rico needs 2 because of capacity.
13 CVRP. Depot per warehouse; 30 packages; 3,000 km; 8 vans. One package = one order. Warehouse capacity ≠ van capacity.
14 GLS. Construct → local search → penalise high-utility arcs at a local minimum → continue on the augmented cost → 10 s. Dallas: NN breaks the limit.
15 Routing results. Fair: −0.12% vs standard (both feasible). Not fair: −18.2% vs NN (infeasible). Small instances explain the small gain.
16 Forecast impact. Same origin and same held-out 13 weeks. CONUS −29.5%; Puerto Rico +21.6% because a transitional week inflated units → a 3rd warehouse. The oracle is a yardstick, not a method.
17 Sensitivity. U-shape with its minimum at 6. Cheaper sites → more sites; cheaper km → fewer sites. Puerto Rico breaks at +10%. Time-limited runs show gaps.
18 Dashboard. 8 pages; what-if re-solves CFLP + CVRP live; streamlit run dashboard/app.py.
19 Conclusion. Exact optimisation dominated; forecasting helps only when unbiased. Limitations: simulated costs, approximate distances, short post-break history, sequential optimisation.

Why these methods?

  • CFLP over K-Means. The CFLP prices fixed cost, capacity and the warehouse count. K-Means minimises geometry only. Measured: K-Means is +0.85% (CONUS) and +27.8% (Puerto Rico).
  • CBC + Pyomo. Open-source, gives a proven optimum with a gap, and an algebraic model that is readable and testable (it matches brute force on toy instances).
  • The GA. A flexible benchmark for when a MILP becomes intractable. Here it quantifies heuristic sub-optimality against a certified optimum.
  • OR-Tools GLS. A strong metaheuristic for CVRP. The comparison with greedy descent isolates the value of the metaheuristic itself.
  • Simple forecasters. Demand is near-stationary noise, and the decisive issue is a regime shift. Model selection was by measured zone-total WAPE.
  • Complete-linkage zones. A guaranteed maximum diameter, verified exactly. K-Means cannot bound zone size.

Likely professor questions

Why remove cancelled orders?
They never ship (all are 'Shipping canceled'), so keeping them would overstate demand.
Why aggregate items into orders?
One order = one delivery. Rows are items, so using them would triple-count trips.
Why demand zones?
The CFLP needs demand at locations. Zones of bounded size make it tractable (312 CONUS points).
Why forecast orders and units separately?
Orders drive transport cost and units drive capacity. Their ratio changed from about 6.5 to 1 at the break.
Why didn't XGBoost win?
Sparse, noisy counts around a level leave little nonlinear signal, and the post-break shift can't be learned from pre-break data.
What is a binary decision variable?
A 0/1 variable: open or not (Yj), assigned or not (Xij).
How does CBC prove optimality?
LP relaxations and cuts give a lower bound. When the best feasible cost meets the bound, the gap is 0 and the solution is optimal.
What if capacity is exceeded?
It's forbidden. In experiments, a penalised slack reports unserved units explicitly, at $10,000/unit.
How does the chromosome work?
37 bits, one per candidate site. 1 = open. Repair keeps the number of open sites between 4 and 12.
Why a GA if the CFLP is exact?
As a benchmark, and for problem variants where the MILP is intractable.
Why can the GA be worse?
It's stochastic, has no bound, and can stall in local optima (mean +1.71%).
CVRP vs TSP?
TSP is one vehicle with no capacity. CVRP adds capacity, so it must also partition stops across vehicles.
Why return to the depot?
To reload. It also makes every route a closed tour in the model.
How does GLS escape local minima?
It penalises frequent long arcs, which changes the landscape so the search moves on. The final choice uses the true cost.
Why only 0.12% from GLS?
12–36 stops per depot with a binding length limit, so greedy descent is already near-optimal.
Why is the NN comparison not fair?
NN breaks the 3,000 km limit on 5 routes, so it isn't a valid plan.
Why did forecasting help CONUS but hurt Puerto Rico?
CONUS was nearly unbiased. Puerto Rico units were +20.4% (transitional week), which bought a whole extra warehouse.
What is a perfect-information benchmark?
A plan built on the realised demand: the best possible plan in hindsight, used to measure regret.
Are these actual costs and routes?
No. Costs are documented assumptions, distances are great-circle × 1.2, and lines are straight segments.
Could a company use this?
The method, yes. It's configuration-driven, so you'd plug in real costs, capacities, road times and time windows.
Why does $18,348 differ from $18,497?
Same solution: $18,348 is the solver objective (opening + transport), and $18,497 adds a constant $148.90 handling cost.
Were all solves optimal?
All main solves, yes. 6 of 34 CONUS sensitivity solves and one Experiment C plan hit 120 s. They're reported as incumbents with their gaps.
Honesty points to volunteer: the sequential (not joint) optimisation; the simulated costs; the approximate distances; the Puerto Rico forecast bias; the small GLS gain; and that XGBoost was not selected.
