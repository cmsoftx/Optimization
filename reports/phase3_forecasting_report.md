# Phase 3: Demand Forecasting

_Generated by `python main.py forecast` (`src/forecasting.py`). Every number is computed from the data. No forecasting output is typed in by hand._

## 1. Set-up

* **Series:** one per demand zone. CONUS has 312 zones and Puerto Rico 25. Hawaii is analysis-only and is not forecast. Targets are **orders** (deliveries) and **units** (`Order Item Quantity`), forecast by separate models.
* **Weekly (primary):** complete Sunday–Saturday weeks, 2015-01-04 → 2018-01-27 (160 weeks). The week of 2017-10-01 starts exactly at the distribution break. **Monthly (alternative):** 37 complete months.
* **Chronological splits.** Hyper-parameters are chosen on validation. Models are then refit on train + validation and scored once on test:

| split | granularity | train | validation | test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pre_break | W | 2015-04-05 → 2016-12-31 (91) | 2017-01-01 → 2017-06-03 (22) | 2017-06-04 → 2017-09-30 (17) |
| post_break | W | 2015-04-05 → 2017-06-03 (113) | 2017-06-04 → 2017-09-30 (17) | 2017-10-01 → 2018-01-27 (17) |
| pre_break | M | 2015-07-01 → 2016-12-31 (18) | 2017-01-01 → 2017-05-31 (5) | 2017-06-01 → 2017-09-30 (4) |
| post_break | M | 2015-07-01 → 2017-05-31 (23) | 2017-06-01 → 2017-09-30 (4) | 2017-10-01 → 2018-01-31 (4) |

* **Evaluation modes:**
  * `one_step` predicts period *t* from actuals up to *t−1* (walk-forward).
  * `horizon` forecasts the whole test window from its origin, recursively, without seeing any test actuals. **This is the mode that produces CFLP inputs.**
  * `post_break_adapted` lets the models observe the first 4 post-break weeks, then forecasts the remaining 13 weeks with three regime handlings: `none`, `recalibrated` (network-level factor = actual ÷ predicted over the observed weeks) and `regime_reset` (baselines that use only post-break or share information).
* **Approaches** (identical inputs and splits):
  1. **Historical average**: zone's expanding mean of all past periods.
  2. **Moving average**: mean of the last *k* periods, with *k* ∈ [4, 8, 13] chosen on validation.
  3. **Random Forest**: 300 trees, a 4-setting grid.
  4. **XGBoost**: an 8-setting grid (objective × depth × min_child_weight), with early stopping.
  Both ML models are **global**: one model per target, pooled over all zones of both networks, with an `is_pr` flag.
* **Features (29, weekly):** `orders_lag1`, `orders_lag2`, `orders_lag3`, `orders_lag4`, `orders_rmean4`, `orders_rmean8`, `orders_rmean13`, `orders_expmean`, `net_orders_lag1`, `net_orders_rmean4`, `units_lag1`, `units_lag2`, `units_lag3`, `units_lag4`, `units_rmean4`, `units_rmean8`, `units_rmean13`, `units_expmean`, `net_units_lag1`, `net_units_rmean4`, `orders_rstd13`, `orders_zero_frac13`, `zone_share13`, `units_per_order13`, `net_units_per_order`, `centroid_lat`, `centroid_lon`, `is_pr`, `period_of_year`.
* **Leakage controls:**
  * Every feature for period *t* is computed from columns < *t* through prefix sums, in one function shared by training, one-step and recursive forecasting.
  * `tests/test_phase3.py` perturbs values at and after *t* and asserts that no feature at ≤ *t* changes.
  * It also checks that recursive features equal batch features, and that horizon forecasts are unaffected by test actuals.
  * Zone tiers are fixed from pre-break training data.
  * Calendar features are known in advance.
  * No feature encodes the break date. A real-time forecaster could not have known it.
* **Metrics:**
  * MAE, RMSE, WAPE = Σ|e|/Σy, and bias = Σe/Σy.
  * **MAPE is reported only for network-level weekly totals**, where every actual is > 0. At zone level most periods are zero, which makes MAPE undefined.
  * An **oracle reference** predicts each zone's *actual* mean over the window. It is not a forecast. It marks the irreducible noise floor of zone-week counts.

## 6. Model selection

**Rule:** per (target, network), pick the (model, regime handling) with the lowest **WAPE of zone totals over the 13-week post-break adapted test window**. That is the quantity the CFLP consumes, in the regime it must plan for.

**How the rule was arrived at.** The first implementation selected on *zone-week* WAPE. Its result (CONUS orders → historical average without regime handling, bias -0.175) exposed a flaw. Zone-week MAE/WAPE is minimised by the conditional *median*, and for sparse counts (most zone-weeks are 0 or 1) the median is near zero. That criterion therefore rewards systematic under-forecasting. The CFLP needs unbiased zone *rates*, so the criterion was changed to zone-total WAPE. Both views are shown below for transparency. The brief asks for selection on test results. With about 15 candidates the optimism from selecting on test is small, but it is not zero.

**XGBoost is not selected for any target or network.** Simpler methods match or beat it on this data, as the noise-floor analysis predicts. The demand is close to stationary noise around a zone level, so there is little nonlinear structure for boosting to exploit. In addition, the decisive problem after the break is a *level and location shift*, which no model trained on pre-break data can learn. It can only be handled by recalibration or a regime reset.


### How XGBoost works (as used here)

**Model.** The forecast is an additive ensemble of $K$ regression trees,
$$\hat y_i=\phi(\mathbf x_i)=\sum_{k=1}^{K} f_k(\mathbf x_i),\qquad f_k\in\mathcal F,$$
where each tree $f_k$ maps a feature vector (lags, rolling means, shares, …) to a leaf
with weight $w_{q(\mathbf x)}$.

**Regularised objective.**
$$\mathcal L=\sum_i l(y_i,\hat y_i)+\sum_k\Omega(f_k),\qquad
\Omega(f)=\gamma T+\tfrac12\lambda\lVert w\rVert^2+\alpha\lVert w\rVert_1 ,$$
where $T$ is the number of leaves. The loss $l$ was chosen on validation from
squared error $(y-\hat y)^2$ and the Poisson deviance
$l=\hat\mu-y\log\hat\mu$ with $\hat\mu=e^{\hat y}$. Poisson suits non-negative counts such as
orders. Here $\lambda=1$ and $\alpha=0$. Tree depth and `min_child_weight` are tuned.

**Gradient boosting (second-order).** Trees are added greedily. At round $t$, the loss is
expanded to second order around the current prediction:
$$\mathcal L^{(t)}\approx\sum_i\big[g_i f_t(\mathbf x_i)+\tfrac12 h_i f_t^2(\mathbf x_i)\big]+\Omega(f_t),\quad
g_i=\partial_{\hat y}l,\; h_i=\partial^2_{\hat y}l .$$
For a fixed tree structure, with $G_j=\sum_{i\in I_j}g_i$ and $H_j=\sum_{i\in I_j}h_i$ over the rows in leaf $j$:
$$w_j^*=-\frac{G_j}{H_j+\lambda},\qquad
\text{score}=-\tfrac12\sum_j\frac{G_j^2}{H_j+\lambda}+\gamma T .$$
A split is kept only if its gain
$\tfrac12\big[\tfrac{G_L^2}{H_L+\lambda}+\tfrac{G_R^2}{H_R+\lambda}-\tfrac{(G_L+G_R)^2}{H_L+H_R+\lambda}\big]-\gamma$
is positive. Each new tree is shrunk by the learning rate $\eta=0.05$:
$\hat y^{(t)}=\hat y^{(t-1)}+\eta f_t$.

**Regularisation in practice.** The settings are: $\lambda$ (L2 on leaf weights), `max_depth`,
`min_child_weight` (minimum $H_j$ in a leaf), row subsampling 0.8, column subsampling 0.8,
shrinkage $\eta$, and **early stopping**. The number of trees is the round with the best
validation MAE, with patience of 100 rounds. That number is then reused when the model is refit
on train + validation.

**Feature importance.** *Gain* is the total split gain (the formula above) contributed by a
feature, normalised to sum to 1. *Weight* is how often a feature is used to split. *Cover* is the
total Hessian of the rows it splits. Gain-based importance is biased towards high-cardinality,
correlated features, so **permutation importance** is also reported. It is the rise in test-set MAE
when one feature's column is randomly shuffled (5 repeats), which measures the feature's real
contribution to accuracy.

**How the CFLP (Phase 4) will use it.** For demand zone *i* (located at its centroid):

## 8. Limitations

* Only 17 post-break weeks exist. Adapted results rest on 13 test weeks, and the forward forecast extrapolates a regime observed for 4 months.
* Zone-week demand is mostly zeros and ones. Point accuracy is bounded by count noise (see the oracle). The CFLP needs zone *rates*, which aggregate well.
* The global ML models are trained almost entirely on pre-break data. They can only adapt through lag features or recalibration, not by learning the new regime.
* Recursive multi-step forecasts can drift. The 13-week horizon results above measure that effect.
