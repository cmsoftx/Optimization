_Generated by `python main.py preprocess`. Every number is computed from the data. Approved scope: CONUS is the primary network, Puerto Rico a separate network, Hawaii is for analysis only, and international destinations are never routed._

* For real cities (all except the catch-all Caguas) the median customer coordinate lies **1.6 to 8.9 km** from the public city centre, and the median customer lies 0.0–15.7 km from their city's median point. Coordinates are city-level points, not exact addresses.
* **Caguas, PR is a catch-all label.** 7,650 customers carry it, but their coordinates spread across the whole island (90th percentile 35.48 km from the median). In Puerto Rico the coordinates, not the city name, define location.
* **Destinations are not geographically linked to customers.** Chi-square tests of `destination_domestic` against customer location:

| test | chi2 | dof | p_value | cramers_v | overall_domestic_share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| destination_domestic vs network | 6.80 | 2 | 0.03 | 0.01 | 0.13 |
| destination_domestic vs customer_state (CONUS) | 58.23 | 43 | 0.06 | 0.04 | 0.13 |

  Cramér's V = 0.010–0.039 is a negligible effect. 9.6%–12.8% of orders are "domestic" in every network, whatever the customer's state or network. Real shipping would show a strong dependence, so using customer coordinates as demand points (approved scope) is the only geographically coherent choice. Setting `preprocessing.destination_scope: domestic_only` in `config.yaml` restricts demand to the domestic-destination subset if a stricter reading is wanted.

## 3. Demand zones

Validated unique coordinates are clustered per network with **complete-linkage hierarchical clustering** (chord distance on the unit sphere, cut at the configured diameter). Complete linkage guarantees that *every pair* of customers in a zone is within the diameter. The exact check below recomputes all pairwise great-circle distances. The method is deterministic (no random seed).

| network | zones | customers | coordinates | orders | units | median_zone_units | max_radius_km | max_pairwise_km | diameter_limit_km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CONUS | 312 | 12,204 | 4,107 | 37,617 | 219,308 | 300.50 | 46.55 | 49.74 | 50 |
| Puerto Rico | 25 | 7,700 | 7,381 | 23,975 | 140,273 | 256.00 | 7.34 | 10.00 | 10 |
| Hawaii | 6 | 137 | 28 | 419 | 2,384 | 440.50 | 4.58 | 8.10 | 10 |

Zone centroids are **unit-weighted** means of the member coordinates. Zone IDs are ranked by units within each network. The diameters (CONUS 50 km, Puerto Rico 10 km, Hawaii 10 km) are modelling choices. They keep the CFLP tractable (I = 312 CONUS zones) while staying small relative to inter-city distances.

## 5. Historical demand patterns

### 5.1 The October 2017 distribution change

| network | regime | calendar_days | orders | units | orders_per_day | units_per_order | items_per_order | units_per_item | customers | share_customers_seen_before_break | categories | top_category |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CONUS | pre_2017_10 | 1,004 | 32,716 | 214,193 | 32.59 | 6.55 | 3.00 | 2.18 | 7,348 |  | 32 | Cleats |
| CONUS | post_2017_10 | 123 | 4,901 | 5,115 | 39.85 | 1.04 | 1.02 | 1.03 | 4,901 | 0.01 | 31 | Video Games |
| Hawaii | pre_2017_10 | 1,004 | 364 | 2,329 | 0.36 | 6.40 | 2.93 | 2.19 | 82 |  | 26 | Cleats |
| Hawaii | post_2017_10 | 123 | 55 | 55 | 0.45 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 55 | 0.00 | 17 | Video Games |
| Puerto Rico | pre_2017_10 | 1,004 | 20,919 | 137,053 | 20.84 | 6.55 | 3.00 | 2.19 | 4,676 |  | 32 | Cleats |
| Puerto Rico | post_2017_10 | 123 | 3,056 | 3,220 | 24.85 | 1.05 | 1.02 | 1.03 | 3,056 | 0.01 | 31 | Video Games |

* **Orders per day rise** (CONUS 32.6 → 39.8), but **units per order fall about 6.3x** (6.55 → 1.04). Orders become single-item, single-unit.
* **The customers are new:** only 0.9% of CONUS customers ordering from 2017-10 had ordered before.
* **The product mix is replaced:** top category Cleats → Video Games (see `phase2_category_shift.png`).
* **The spatial pattern is preserved.** Zone order shares before and after correlate with Spearman ρ = 0.93 (CONUS), 0.71 (PR) and 0.93 (HI).

**Interpretation:** the break looks like a change of catalogue and customer cohort, not a change in *where* demand comes from. Two consequences follow:
1. **Unit-based demand cannot be extrapolated across the break.** A model trained on 2015–2017-09 units will overshoot post-break units about 5.1x (CONUS units/day). Forecasting must condition on the regime, or forecast **orders** (deliveries, which stay stable), or both.
2. **Spatial shares are robust,** so warehouse location (which depends on *where* demand is) is far less sensitive to the break than total volume is.

### 5.2 Temporal structure

* Monthly unit CV before the break: CONUS 0.054, PR 0.048. There is **no visible seasonality** or trend (`phase2_monthly_demand.png`, `phase2_weekly_orders.png`).
* Weekday index range (max − min) in CONUS before the break: 0.018. There is **no weekday effect** (`phase2_day_of_week.png`).
* Calendar features will therefore add little signal. The honest expectation for Phase 3 is that the zone's own demand level (lags, rolling means) dominates. XGBoost must be compared with a moving-average baseline, which may perform similarly.

### 5.3 Sparsity

| network | zero % (zone-day) | zero % (zone-week) | zero % (zone-month) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CONUS | 91.10 | 64.80 | 34.40 |
| Hawaii | 94.00 | 65.80 | 21.60 |
| Puerto Rico | 85.30 | 66.70 | 35.60 |

Most zone-days are zero, so daily zone forecasts are not meaningful. Phase 3 will compare **weekly** and **monthly** zone-level targets (and network-level totals). The CFLP consumes a per-zone demand rate for the planning horizon.

## 7. Privacy

`Customer Fname`, `Customer Lname`, `Customer Street`, `Customer Email`, `Customer Password` and `Customer Zipcode` are removed before any file is written. `_assert_no_pii` blocks a write if a PII column reappears, and a unit test checks the written files. `customer_id` is the dataset's pseudonymous key. Coordinates are city-level points (see §2), not street addresses. Processed files stay git-ignored. Only aggregated zone-level tables are committed.
