## 5. Geography: what the coordinates actually describe

The data dictionary says `Latitude`/`Longitude` are the *"location of store"*. Empirically:
* Each customer has exactly one coordinate (0 violations), and coordinates cluster by `Customer City`/`Customer State`. They behave as a **customer-side (US) location**.
* Customers are only in EE. UU. and Puerto Rico (`EE. UU.` = USA).
* `Order City`/`Order Country` (the delivery *destination*) span 164 countries and **have no coordinates**. Every `Market` mixes US and Puerto Rico customers:

| Market | EE. UU. | Puerto Rico |
|---|---|---|
| Africa | 7,416 | 4,198 |
| Europe | 30,516 | 19,736 |
| LATAM | 31,699 | 19,895 |
| Pacific Asia | 25,528 | 15,732 |
| USCA | 15,987 | 9,812 |

The destination fields are therefore not geographically linked to the customer coordinates (a US customer can have an order destined for Indonesia). This is a known property of this synthetic dataset.

From **2017-10** onward the data-generating process changes: units/month fall from about 11,463 to about 2,124, items per order fall from about 3.00 to about 1.00, the number of categories sold per month falls from 24 to 8–20, while orders per month *rise* (CONUS-customer orders go from about 1,064 to about 1,290). The forecasting phase must treat this regime change explicitly. It must not use the tail months naively as a test set.

Also note that throughout the whole history each month contains orders for only 1–5 of the 5 `Market` values (median 1). Markets rotate month by month, which confirms that `Market`/`Order Region` are synthetic labels and should not drive spatial demand.

## 10. Modelling decision implied by the inspection

Only the customer-side coordinates exist, and the destination fields are geographically incoherent with them. The recommended scope for the network design is therefore:

* **Demand points = customer coordinates** (`Latitude`/`Longitude`), aggregated by order and then into spatial demand zones.
* **Primary network = contiguous US (CONUS)**: 110,286 rows, 12,627 customers. Truck routing is physically meaningful here.
* **Puerto Rico** (68,775 rows, an island unreachable by truck from the mainland) is modelled as a separate sub-network or reported separately.
* Hawaii (1,187 rows) is reported only. Alaska has no records.
* Records failing the coordinate checks are excluded from the spatial model and counted in the data-quality report.

## 11. Privacy

`Customer Fname`, `Customer Lname` and `Customer Street` contain personal data. Email and password are already masked. These columns are dropped from every processed output, and the raw files are git-ignored.
